Use cases

When to use
mezzanine finance.

Development mezzanine
Top up your senior development facility to reduce your equity contribution. Particularly effective on larger schemes where equity is scarce.
Investment mezzanine
Increase leverage on commercial or residential investment acquisitions — where senior debt alone doesn't provide sufficient purchase funds.
Acquisition bridging top-up
Use mezzanine alongside a first-charge bridge to fund a higher LTV acquisition than standard bridging will support.

Structured preferred equity returns as an alternative to traditional mezzanine debt — aligned interest, flexible structure.

Common questions

Everything you need to know
about mezzanine finance.

What is mezzanine finance?+
Mezzanine finance sits between senior debt (first charge) and equity in the capital stack. It takes a second charge position and is priced higher than senior debt. It allows borrowers to achieve higher overall leverage with less equity.
When should I use mezzanine instead of more senior debt?+
When your senior lender won't increase their facility but you need additional funds. Mezzanine is often used in development to reduce the equity requirement, making deals viable that wouldn't otherwise stack up.
What's the difference between mezzanine and a second charge bridge?+
A second charge bridge is typically a short-term facility behind a first charge. Mezzanine is specifically used in development or investment transactions as a layer of the capital stack — more structurally complex.
Do senior and mezzanine lenders need to agree to work together?+
Yes — mezzanine lenders require an inter-creditor agreement with the senior lender. Our underwriting team manages this process and has established relationships with lenders who work together regularly.
